# TASK 13 — Central Fringe Populations and a Genuine Exponential-Sum Test

## Starting point

TASK 12 proved exact labelled coincidence laws and a new infinite class with one central covering runner, but disproved both naive innermost-boundary descent statements. It identified residues \(1\) and \(q-1\) as the asymmetric fringe populations. It also found that Weil/Kloosterman estimates do not directly apply: the current formulas contain affine interval phases, not complete inverse-phase sums.

## Objective

Control the two labelled fringe populations around the innermost central pair, or rigorously derive (or rule out) a genuine Kloosterman-type reduction.

## Required work

1. For every runner, derive exact distances from \(W\pm b_*\) to its nearest labelled block as functions of \(v_i\bmod q\), \(b_i\), and \(b_*\).
2. Formulate and test a two-sided fringe inequality sufficient for a zero segment; preserve runner labels and endpoint pairing.
3. Classify a new infinite family allowing residue \(q-1\), multiple velocities divisible by \(q\), or both.
4. Test whether the bad fringe incidences can be parametrized over a prime field by a complete phase \(av+bv^{-1}\). If so, state the exact sum and verify every hypothesis of the Weil bound, including completion/truncation errors. If not, prove the algebraic obstruction for the attempted parametrization.
5. Do not call geometric sums, interval Fourier coefficients, or composite congruence counts “Kloosterman sums.” Distinguish complete sums, incomplete sums, and Kloosterman paths.
6. Extend exact M-free experiments beyond TASK 12 and preserve the smallest counterexample to every proposed fringe inequality.
7. Update `research/covering/` with proofs, deterministic results, claim statuses, and the next narrow barrier.
