# TASK 23 — Phase-Sensitive Multiplicity Surplus

## Starting point

TASK 22 gives exact all-triple identities weighted by the pointwise bad
multiplicity $m(z)$. Under hypothetical full coverage their difference is
nonnegative, so phase-blind averaging cannot force a safe point. The
largest-mass canonical triple rule is explicitly disproved.

## Objective

Determine whether the divisor-compatible phases force a quantitative
restriction on

$$
\Sigma=\sum_{z\bmod L}(m(z)-1){N-m(z)\choose3},
$$

or find an LRC-derived canonical grid disproving a proposed restriction.

## Required work

1. Express the multiplicity histogram and $\Sigma$ through labelled CRT
   intersections, proving every combinatorial coefficient.
2. Separate the multiplicity-one contribution from the overlap surplus
   $m\ge2$ and determine exactly which intersection orders control it.
3. Use $(H_i,a_i,c_i,\rho_i)$ and their divisor restrictions; do not replace
   the strips by arbitrary subsets.
4. Test whether divisor alignment $g_i\mid b_i$, valuation alignment, or a
   stated phase-dispersion condition yields a strict theorem.
5. Search the lcm period, never $qW$, for covered grids and for counterexamples
   to every candidate surplus inequality.
6. Include $(1,3,4,5)$, all TASK 14 obstructions, the TASK 22 certificate
   $(1,4,5,6,9)$, and every applicable required family.
7. Store compact phase records and a multiplicity histogram for every decisive
   counterexample.
8. Update global status and create `TASK24.md` only after a theorem,
   counterexample, or sharply isolated obstruction is obtained.
